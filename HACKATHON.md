# Couple Order Shop · Hackathon Submission

> A shop with exactly two customers. It turns "I'd like you to watch a movie with me"
> — the sentence that feels too needy to say out loud — into an order:
> write the wish down, put a price on it, and pay for it with everyday attention.

---

## 1. The problem

The hard part of a relationship is rarely that nobody wants to give. It is that
**you don't know what the other person wants right now**, and that **asking feels
like too much**.

Nothing on a phone solves this. A to-do list is too cold to hold "hug me for ten
minutes." A request in a chat thread is buried under three days of messages.
Most couple apps do check-ins, anniversary countdowns and day counters — they
**record the relationship without moving anything inside it**.

So we bet on one metaphor: **ordering**. A menu turns "I want" into something you
can point at. A price makes asking legitimate instead of needy. Accepting and
finishing an order gives the giving a receipt. That interaction is understood by
everyone alive, and we moved it, unchanged, in between two people.

## 2. What it is

Five screens — **Shop / Tasks / Orders / Memories / Us** — two phones, one
six-digit couple code.

**40 wishes on the menu**, in four categories:

| Category | Count | Price range | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food | 13 | 28–78 | Fruit Tea, Full Cup 28 · Hot Pot, Steaming 68 |
| Care | 10 | 48–118 | A Ten-Minute Hug 48 · One Phone-Free Hour 78 · An Hour of Listening 98 |
| Dates | 11 | 60–188 | A Walk, Hand in Hand 60 · Go Watch the Sunset 78 · A Weekend Away 188 |
| Limited | 6 | 120–360 | One Wilful Wish 188 · A Whole Day, Your Call 228 |

**The sweet-heart coin economy** is the skeleton the whole product hangs on:

- **One wallet per person.** Your partner cannot spend your coins. Both sides open
  at 8, and pairing pays each of you 20 more.
- Coins come from **8 daily tasks plus a check-in**: say good morning or goodnight
  properly (+1), pay one real compliment (+2), one phone-free hour, and so on.
  At most 8 a day and 29 more a week per person — 85 in a perfect week, 30–50 for
  normal participation. So "A Weekend Away, 188" takes real saving while "A
  Ten-Minute Hug, 48" is affordable today. **Price is priority**, and that was designed.
- An order is paid from **the sender's own** wallet. A decline refunds them in full;
  the recipient's balance never moves.
- **A limited coupon can be used once per couple, ever.** Once spent it stays greyed
  out in the shop forever.
- **Wishes of your own**: all three non-limited categories accept them, 8–400 coins,
  either person can edit or retire one, 30 per couple. Limited coupons stay a fixed
  list on purpose — a "once in a lifetime" you issued yourself is one nobody can keep for you.

**Memories** holds a timeline that is never truncated (grouped by month, so early
wishes are not quietly dropped) and **11 milestones**: 100/365/520/1,000 days
together, 1/10/50/100 wishes finished, 7/30/100-day check-in streaks.

**English and Chinese, per device**, so two people can read the same shop in two
languages. The line is drawn here: **the interface translates, and the couple's own
words never do.** What you wrote — "今晚想吃你煮的面" — should not be handed to your
partner in another language.

running under. The template's version is now a design preview, lazily loaded behind
`?preview=1`, and the README says plainly that its keyboard and safe areas are
approximations that prove nothing about a device.

## 4. The four things we most want looked at

**1. The economy lives in the database, not the front end.**
The price of a couple's own wish is read server-side from `custom_menu_items`; whatever
the client claims a wish costs is ignored. `insert/update/delete` on `orders` has been
revoked from `anon` and `authenticated`, so placing an order and moving it through its
states can only happen inside two SECURITY DEFINER functions. "You only ever see your own
balance" is enforced by an RLS policy on `profiles` that admits `user_id = auth.uid()`
and nothing else — and `get_partner_status()`, the function that shows you how your
partner's week is going, **does not and must not carry a balance**.

**2. `npm run test:sql` proves the grants are intact without touching a database.**
It replays all 14 migrations in filename order and computes the final set of policies and
grants, watching for one specific accident: **a policy added while its grant was forgotten**.
Postgres checks table privileges before RLS, so a miss is an immediate permission denied.

> That rule was written in blood. `20260912000000_read_grants.sql` adds the select grants
> that four core tables **never had**. Without it, every screen after login returned
> permission denied — you could not read your own balance. The whole app was shut.

**3. Every migration has to be safe to run twice**, and `npm run check:migrations` verifies
it statically on every `npm test`: policies must `drop policy if exists` first, tables must be
`if not exists`, added columns need a guard, `create function` must be `or replace`. The
reasoning is concrete — any statement that only works on an empty database will blow up
halfway through the first re-run and leave the schema half-changed.

**4. Contrast and touch targets are measured, not eyeballed.**
`tests/accessibility.spec.ts` measures 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and 44×44 touch targets
on all five screens, against what the browser actually paints. The date-logic unit tests run
**twice, once in US Eastern and once in Beijing** — task periods are keyed to `Asia/Shanghai`
and front end and database share one period key (`src/lib/date.ts` and `public.app_today()`),
so a couple in two time zones still agrees on what day it is.

Task honesty works the same way: the photo, finished-order and date tasks are verified
server-side against `orders.completed_at`. The other five have nothing in the data to prove
them, so **they run on the honour system and we say so**, rather than pretending to check.

## 5. Demo script (2 minutes, two phones)

| Time | Action | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Two phones side by side, each picks its own side | Identity is per device, not an account switch |
| 0:20 | A creates the shop and gets a six-digit code; B enters it → both get +20 | Pairing means you can order today, not in three days |
| 0:40 | A orders "Movie Night" for 88; it appears on B's phone | Realtime, not polling |
| 1:00 | B declines with a reason → A's 88 comes back, B's balance unchanged | The wallets are separate |
| 1:20 | A writes a wish of their own; it shows up on B's phone | Custom wishes are in the realtime publication too |
| 1:40 | **B switches to 中文** | The interface changes; **the wish A just wrote does not** |
| 2:00 | Memories: milestones and the untruncated timeline | There is something here in six months |

## 6. What it still doesn't do

- Membership and payments are **off by default** (`VITE_MEMBERSHIP_ENABLED=0`), as are phone
  and Apple sign-in. Unconfigured paths are hidden entirely rather than shown and then failing.
- 5 of the 8 tasks cannot be verified from data and run on the honour system.
- Local-only mode has no photo album, so the photo task is not checked there.
- Web Push needs an HTTPS deployment and both iPhones to "Add to Home Screen".
- The Supabase free tier has no `pg_cron`, so `prune_operational_data()` has to be called
  manually or by an external scheduler.

## 7. Run it yourself

```bash
git clone https://github.com/constana-an/cursor-grok.git
cd cursor-grok
npm install
npm run dev     # with no environment variables it runs in local-only mode — one phone walks the whole thing
```

```bash
npm test        # runtime integrity → migration replay → dual-timezone units
                # → RLS policies and grants → worker → Playwright
```

Full setup for cloud sync, migrations and push is in [README.md](README.md).
